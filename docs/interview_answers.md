# How I structure a spoken answer about a rule

My problem isn't knowing the material, it's assembling it under pressure: where to start, how to get from one topic to the next, and how to stop. So I use a fixed spine. I memorise the spine, not the answer.

## The spine: Why, Count, Cap, Cross, Clear, Cost

Six beats, one or two sentences each. That's 60 to 90 seconds spoken.

| Beat | What it does | The phrase I use to get into it |
|---|---|---|
| **Why** | The problem the regulator is solving. One sentence. | "The concern is that..." |
| **Count** | How a position is measured. | "The way they measure it is..." |
| **Cap** | What that measurement is compared against, and how the cap itself is set. | "That number is then compared against..." |
| **Cross** | What changes when the threshold is breached. | "Once that ratio crosses..." |
| **Clear** | How the restriction ends. | "It stays there until..." |
| **Cost** | The penalty, and who enforces it. | "And if someone breaches it..." |
| *Close* | One line on what I built and what was hard. | "That's what I built, and the hard part was..." |

Each beat starts from the previous beat's output. That's what makes it sound like one explanation instead of six facts.

## Three rules

1. **Open with the purpose, never a definition or a number.** "The concern is that one firm can corner a stock's derivatives" is an opening. "MWPL is the lower of two quantities" is not: nobody knows yet why they should care.
2. **Never open with a number.** Numbers land once the listener has the shape. Put them in Cap and Cross.
3. **End deliberately.** Finish on Cost, then one line tying it to what I built. Don't trail off and wait to be rescued.

## The shape, shown on something else

Driving, so the structure is visible without the F&O content:

> The concern is that roads are shared, so one driver going too fast puts everyone else at risk. The way they measure it is simple: your speed at a point, caught on camera. That's compared against the posted limit, which isn't arbitrary, it's set from the road type and the conditions. Once you cross it, you don't just pay a fine, you get points added to your licence, so the consequence accumulates. Those points stay on the licence until they expire a few years later. And if you collect enough of them, the licence is suspended outright. The hard part is that cameras only sit at some points on the road, so enforcement is sampled rather than continuous.

Same six beats. Nothing memorised, just the spine walked in order.

## Where I use it

Every teach-back, the weekly interview drills, and the final mock interview. One spine per rule: MWPL and the ban period, the entity limits, intraday monitoring, OTR. By week 6 these become the story sheet in `LEARNING.md`.
